# Hand-Drawn Marker Illustration — Bright Original

Use this version for the approved bright outputs from the conversation, especially the museum afternoon image and the sunset pagodas image the user marked as good. The style should feel like a handmade travel sticker or illustrated postcard: simple, warm, slightly naive, strongly outlined, and pleasant at thumbnail size.

The goal is not to invent a new illustration style each time. Stay close to the approved references: bold black marker contour, clean color blocks, warm textured paper, simple silhouettes, small imperfect details, and a playful hand-drawn mood.

## Core look

- Use a thick, confident near-black marker contour. The line should be bold and slightly uneven, with soft wobble, pooled corners, rounded joins, and a few natural breaks. It should not become thin vector linework or fuzzy sketching.
- Build the subject from clear, readable silhouettes and broad color planes. Recognition should come from the main shape and color relationship before small detail.
- Keep color bright but source-faithful. Sample the important colors from the source, then reduce them to a clean limited palette. Do not add unrelated rainbow colors.
- Keep fills mostly solid and marker-like. A little paper grain is good; dense pencil strokes, scratchy hatching, smearing, or heavy dry-brush across every fill is not.
- Let the warm off-white paper show around the image. The result should feel printed or drawn on paper, not digitally airbrushed.
- Preserve the source's pose, composition, subject identity, and mood. Simplify the scene, but do not replace it with unrelated decorations.

## Canvas, paper, and margins

The paper canvas is part of the style. Do not simply repaint the photo edge-to-edge.

- Default to the approved illustrated-card feeling: a centered scene on warm off-white paper, with visible breathing room on the left, right, top, and bottom.
- For single portrait scenes, museum scenes, landmarks, pets, and travel postcards, prefer a compact portrait card close to the approved examples rather than a tall phone-photo crop. The subject should sit inside the paper, not touch the image edges.
- Keep roughly 8–15% paper margin around the illustrated area when the source allows it. Side margins are especially important; avoid full-bleed compositions unless the user explicitly asks for full-bleed.
- Preserve the source composition as the relationship between objects, not as an exact camera crop or aspect ratio. Reframe photos into a handmade illustration layout when needed.
- Paper texture should remain visible across the whole canvas: subtle warm fiber and grain, not a blank digital white background and not a heavy dirty texture.

## The approved flavor

The approved examples share these traits:

- **Museum/person scenes:** chunky furniture, simple human pose, clean red/brown/cream color blocks, thick black contour, a few interior lines for books, glass, cabinets, and floor. The image stays charming because it is simple and handmade, not because every texture is rendered.
- **Sunset pagoda/city scenes:** broad sunset cloud bands, dark stacked silhouettes, simple roofs and windows, warm glowing accents, slightly naive but still recognizable architecture. The scene keeps its atmosphere through big shapes and color contrast.
- **Sticker collections and city scenes:** individual subjects remain readable, separated, and lightly imperfect. Text, when requested, is thick rounded handwriting with compact spacing.

## Detail handling

Handle detail gently. Do not turn this into a rigid cleanup pipeline.

- For already clear subjects, keep the normal bright style. Do not over-simplify, over-round, or add extra Q-style treatment.
- For visually dense subjects such as curly fur, ornate buildings, tree foliage, repeated windows, roof tiles, grass, or busy textiles, reduce small repeated detail into grouped shapes. Keep the main silhouette plus a few identity cues.
- Use fewer marks than the photo contains, but keep enough hand-drawn irregularity that the image still feels alive.
- If a dense subject becomes stiff after simplification, lightly soften it with rounder, chunkier shapes and small asymmetries. Use this only as a correction, not as a default effect.

Useful simplifications:

- Fur: keep the face, ears, paws, mouth, and a few grouped tufts; avoid strand-by-strand curls.
- Buildings: keep the main massing, roof tiers, towers, and a few windows or bands; avoid drawing every ornament.
- Foliage and grass: use masses, bands, or a few clusters; avoid leaf-by-leaf or blade-by-blade marks.
- Skies and sunsets: use broad clean bands and cloud shapes, not many blended strokes.

## Color guidance

Brightness comes from clean contrast and solid pigment, not from adding many hues.

Typical useful colors include:

- warm paper `#F0EDE3`
- charcoal black `#17191A`
- dark blue-grey `#465663`
- wood brown `#70432D`
- burnt orange `#C66A2E`
- ochre yellow `#D79C38`
- pale turquoise `#9CCBC5`
- brick red `#C6282D`
- quiet cream `#F4E5B9`

Adapt these to the source. For a grey-and-white animal, keep the coat grey, ivory, and charcoal, with only real source accents such as a collar, harness, toy, or background color.

Color correction is scene-specific:

- Keep the current source-faithful color treatment for clear daylight scenes, warm interiors, people, food, and already balanced illustrations.
- For dark backlit or sunset scenes, do not deepen every plane together. Keep the sky's orange, coral, and ochre clean and luminous; separate the foreground into charcoal, dark brown, muted plum, and a few warm window lights so the silhouette stays readable without becoming muddy.
- Lift dark masses only enough to preserve the marker illustration's color separation. Do not wash out the scene or turn a naturally dark subject into pastel.
- Do not apply a global saturation, brightness, or contrast adjustment across unrelated scenes.

## Composition and lettering

- Prefer a compact illustrated scene, single sticker, postcard, or sticker-sheet item on warm paper.
- Keep proportions slightly handmade and imperfect, but do not force mascot proportions unless the source naturally calls for it.
- If captions are requested, use thick rounded handwritten uppercase Latin, with a smaller local-language line beneath when useful. Text should feel drawn with the same marker as the contour.
- When working from an approved sticker/card reference that contains a caption, retain or restore that caption when the user asks for the same treatment. Reproduce the supplied wording exactly, place it below the illustration with comfortable paper margin, and do not invent replacement text. If no caption is supplied or requested, leave the paper clean.

## Prompt language

Use language such as: “approved bright hand-drawn marker sticker style, warm off-white textured paper with subtle visible grain, centered illustrated card composition with 8–15% paper margins, thick uneven near-black contour, clean opaque marker fills, source-faithful limited palette, simple readable silhouettes, a few imperfect interior marks, handmade travel postcard feeling.”

For photo conversion, add: “preserve the source subject, pose, object relationships, mood, and source-derived colors; reframe the photo into a compact paper-card illustration instead of copying the full phone-photo crop; keep visible side margins and warm paper grain; simplify repeated detail into grouped shapes only when needed; keep the result close to the approved museum and sunset pagoda references.”

## Avoid

Avoid thin vector outlines, smooth digital gradients, photorealism, glossy 3D, edge-to-edge phone-photo repainting, cropped-off paper margins, blank digital white backgrounds, over-rendered texture, colored-pencil appearance, scratchy hatching, dense dry-brush everywhere, rainbow color expansion, invented neon accents, excessive Q-style mascot distortion, over-precise architectural drafting, line-by-line fur, window-by-window buildings, leaf-by-leaf foliage, and unrelated decorative additions.
